Part Two: the 35 Q&A topics

The book answers real readers' and audience questions, alphabetically. Below: each question as asked and the answer's key points in Maltz's wording.

TopicQuestion askedWhat the book answersPages
AngerAnger — sudden and often — why? Is it necessary?"One of the great destructive forces within us." Anger is "an explosive kind of frustration". Three ways to stand up to it: (1) look in the mirror, turn the snarling face into your confident face and laugh out loud; (2) walk off pent-up energy — "Walk, walk, walk. Forgive, forgive, forgive"; (3) write the nastiest letter you can, re-read it (a "decompression chamber"), then tear it up173–174
CompassionWhat is the nicest word?Compassion, "because it incorporates love". It means giving your courage, confidence and self-respect to others. "A full-time job that needs no time." You cannot give it to others without compassion for yourself175–176
DisciplineWhat is discipline?Story of Semmelweis vs. authority. Discipline "must be creative, not destructive" — a partnership, "a board meeting" between adult and child (or manager and managed). It first requires self-discipline: control fear and anger; use understanding, self-respect, courage176–177
Emotional ScarsA simple guide to heal emotional scars?Don't sell yourself out, bemoan "bad luck" or hide as unworthy. Learn from people who endured and "refused to let emotional scars disfigure them". Ask daily: "How do I, my own plastic surgeon, heal my emotional wounds?" Best tool: the self-image you create177–178
EvilAren't you ignoring evil?"The greatest evil in man is within himself when he makes a blunder and refuses to rise above it." Two forces within: will to survive vs. will to self-destruction; success mechanism vs. failure mechanism. Recognize negative feelings, then turn "a crisis into an opportunity"178–179
ExistentialismHow does psycho-cybernetics relate to existentialism?Sartre's No Exit = "a philosophy of negation". Psycho-cybernetics is the reverse: "not 'no exits'" but "entrances". "Existentialism is a red light! STOP. Psycho-cybernetics is a green light: GO FORWARD"179–180
FriendshipWhat is friendship? How do you make friends?"Not what we take from others, but what we give." To be a friend to others, first be a friend to yourself. Story of four women who can't keep a "poker face": show what is in your heart; a false face makes people wonder what is behind it180–182
FrustrationHow do you overcome frustration?We "jam our creative mechanism" by forcing solutions with the forebrain. Five roadblocks and cures: (1) worry after deciding → worry before, then use past-success confidence; (2) worry about yesterday and tomorrow → "Every day is a complete lifetime"; (3) too many things at once → one thing at a time; (4) problems 24 hours a day → "Sleep on it, not with it"; (5) refusing to relax → relax in "a room of your mind"183–184
FunctioningSteps to function at my best?11 points: we are goal-striving beings; happiness is a goal "like breakfast"; the "happiness habit" vs. the "unhappiness syndrome"; happiness is not earned or deserved; unhappiness "is selfish in that it destroys our worth"185–186
GodIs God dead?Saying God is dead means "something within them is dead". The greatest battlefield is "within himself: the battle with his negative feelings". Tear down the "wall of Jericho" of resentment. "You must first police yourself"186–187
HappinessCan you make a habit of happiness? How to spread it? Definition? Abundant living?8 ingredients: smile; be cheerful (recall a past success); be part of humanity; stop criticizing self and others; don't let opinion color facts; react calmly; if you fail, try again tomorrow; know your capacities. "Typhoid Marys of Happiness" spread it (11 signs). Definition: "a state of mind or habit where we have pleasant thoughts a greater share of the time." "Happiness is the only product in the world that multiplies by division"187–192
HumilityWhat is humility?"Not to make us feel passively insignificant, but actively important." "Not self-denial. It is self-affirmation" — an "emotional thermostat". 8 ingredients: sincerity, understanding, knowledge, capacity to listen, integrity, contentment, yearning, maturity192–193
IdentityHow to keep your identity amid over-conformity? How to know the real you?Some conformity is healthy; over-conformity is agreeing blindly, living by others' standards, "keeping up with the Joneses" — "keep up" with yourself. Ask in the mirror: "Who am I?" Self-respect "is the beginning of your real self"194–195
LonelinessWhy do I feel alone?Three kinds: toward the outside world, toward another person, toward yourself ("by far the worst"). "Loneliness means eviction. You cast yourself away from yourself." "No one can make you lonely without your consent"196–197
MoodinessHow to deal with moodiness?Moods are "emotional cycles" that depend on your self-image. Low periods are "periods of unbelief". Cure: sit, relax, go into "a room of your mind", see and feel past successes197–198
Negative FeelingsHow do you overcome negative feelings?Answered with a hockey player's letter: reliving games in a dark room, he found fear of injury behind every failure, replaced it with remembered goals, went from bench reserve to leading scorer198–200
PressureHow do you live with pressure? Know you are yourself?"First, you must accept your periods of weakness" — otherwise "you have no floor under you to cushion your falls". Success means "the capacity to rise above a problem". You are "a combination of frustration and confidence"200–201
PsychoanalysisCan psycho-cybernetics substitute for psychoanalysis?No. Psychoanalysis "delves into the past"; psycho-cybernetics "into the present". It is "self-analysis without using another party". It treats "the average psychoneurosis" as "just a bad habit". Includes a psychiatrist's letter201–203
Psycho-cyberneticsWhat does the word mean?From Greek kybernetes, "a helmsman". Psycho-cybernetics = "steering your mind to a productive, useful goal". Dalí's painting: "small potato" vs. man "ten feet tall"; the port = peace of mind. "One step beyond… positive thinking, is positive doing"203–204
RelaxationHow does one relax in uncertain times?Five cardinal points: (1) forgive others — "no forgiveness on the installment plan"; (2) forgive yourself; (3) keep up with yourself, not someone else; (4) see yourself at your best; (5) in a quiet room imagine a geyser letting off steam, to "break your electric circuit of distress"204–206
ReligionWhere does religion fit in?"No substitute for religion but it has a spiritual basis." It is "a search for your integrity as a human being" and helps you fulfill yourself "regardless of your religious beliefs"206–208
Self-image13 questions (most important asset; can people change; first step; conceit; limits; conscience; LSD; snobbery; helping an associate; overconfidence; why people keep the same image; peace of mind; college students)Man's most important asset: "His self-image" — "a mental picture, a blueprint". People change if they change their self-image; "this self-image doesn't rule us; we rule it." Each morning in the mirror ask who you want to be today. You are "a mistake maker" and "a mistake breaker". Conceit = "lack of confidence expressing a false sense of superiority". "The strength of our self-image is the strength of our conscience." Snobbery = conceit, emptiness, pettiness. Story of Helen, told she was clumsy at the piano. Peace of mind: make it a goal, escape a little each day, build your self-image208–223
SexHave we false conceptions about sex?Little correlation between real fulfillment and "the ballyhoo of sexual imagery". The fulfilled person "knows how to give and take — without narcissism" and has a healthy self-image223–224
SinYour interpretation of sin?"I don't know what sin is." The great cardinal sin "is not making a mistake but the refusal to rise above a mistake"; the other "is to refuse to forgive others"224–225
SleepA nursing student who can't sleep until 2–4 a.m.First principle: a fixed routine — same bedtime every night. Learn relaxation; control worry. A lukewarm bath is the best artificial aid. Avoid pills if well; don't read before bed. Prepare a relaxed attitude during the evening225–227
Small PotatoI always feel like a small potatoCarlyle: "Society is founded on hero worship." Story of a woman brooding over a tiny lip scar. Recipe for becoming one: coddle regrets, long for the past, complain, stay hurt. Cure: "Turn your back on the failures of yesterday… Live creatively today — with a goal for each day"227–228
SuccessMaking your own success; efficiency; inner drive; slumps; servo-mechanism; most successful manRespect loved ones' wisdom, then your own — "a U.N. of distinct self-images", "your own Columbus". Efficiency = a morning mirror resolution ("I shall remember…" list). Slumps are natural — "The best batter in baseball never hits home runs all the time." Servo-mechanism explained with picking up a pen. Most successful man of the century: "You … if you fulfill yourself." SUCCESS list (see acronyms). "Success means trying, trying, trying, doing, doing, doing, NOW NOW NOW"228–235
TomorrowHow do I overcome procrastination?"No one has ever seen 'tomorrow'." A positive tomorrow habit: postpone hate, insolence, bigotry, envy, malice, indolence, boredom, revenge, slander, lack of faith — "till tomorrow". Become aware of "the ludicrous evasion" of procrastination235–236
TruthHow do you appraise yourself?"We are least objective in appraising ourselves." Our "truths" about ourselves (money, marriage, a long nose) are "distortions". "De-hypnotize yourself"; "give yourself some justice for a change"236–237
UnhappinessHow do you break / make the unhappiness habit?Put up "a mental mirror" in the room of your mind and see yourself as the world sees you; end the habit, substitute the happiness habit. Unhappiness becomes a habit by refusing to let go of yesterday. 8 ingredients to avoid (frown, sad, unfriendly, critical…). "No one can make you unhappy without your consent"237–239
WeaknessWhy do men punish themselves so much?For the "crime" of weakness: self-expectations "stem from fantasy more than from reality" — the "tough guy" who never cries. "Just as you cherish your strengths, you must accept your weaknesses"239–240
WeekendHow to "psych" yourself up on Monday?Use rest periods not to run away from yourself but to know yourself. Every day sit a few minutes "in a quiet room of your mind" and take stock — a "found" weekend, not a "lost" one240–241
WinningHow do you become a winner? A quick formula?Your own "Kentucky Derby": two minutes a day in a quiet room, running for self-respect. Two rules: (1) get tough — get out into the world; (2) set goals, even invisible ones (forgiving a friend, imagining past successes)241–243
WorryWhy should worry be a challenge?Worry should be "a challenge to stand up to our full stature of dignity". Don't carry it from business to home to bed. Five minutes a day of pleasant ideas and memories; "it is just as easy to have the happiness habit as the unhappiness habit"243–244
YesterdayShouldn't we learn from the past?Yes: learn not to repeat mistakes, and use past successes for "instant confidence". But carrying blunders = "fifty extra pounds of continual chronic frustration". Visit the past briefly (a "gallery" in the mind), then return; forget negative feelings "by substitution"244–245

Small potatoA shrunken self-image; Dalí's painting shows man "the size of a small potato" vs. "ten feet tall"204, 216, 227–228

Wall of JerichoThe wall of resentment to tear down so you see yourself "in God's image"187, 207
