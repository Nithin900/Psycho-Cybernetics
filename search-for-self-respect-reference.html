The Search for Self-Respect, at a glance

Look things up: the acrostics Maltz spells out (RESENTMENT, TRANQUILLITY, INTEGRITY, SURVIVAL…), every numbered list, the fourteen Goal Builders and the stories. Book content only, with page numbers.

Fifteen chapters (pp. 3–249). Chapters 1–14 each build one part of self-respect; Chapter 15 gives one Goal Builder exercise for each of them, one a week for fourteen weeks.

The fourteen Goal Builders

Chapter 15: one a week for fourteen weeks, six days on and one off. Each ends with a word written on your mirror (p. 231).

WeekGoal BuilderThe exerciseMirror wordWhat you sayPages
1Self-RespectImagine yourself a snarling, rioting convict; return to reality and ask whether you are really like thatSELF-RESPECT"Today I will try to respect myself. I deserve to respect myself."232–233
2UnderstandingWalk down a street noticing who seems to feel they have a right to be happy and who doesn'tUNDERSTANDING"Do I have rights? Yes. Yes, I do."233–235
3SincerityPicture yourself in the amusement park "fun" mirrors; then ask if you distort yourself emotionallySINCERITYLaugh at your emotional distortions as you would at the fun mirror235–237
4Defeating UncertaintyImagine being cut off at night on a Pacific island in WWII (or another terror); your life could be worseDEFEAT UNCERTAINTY"I will try to face my problems… and not run to hide at the first sign of danger."237–238
5Overcoming ResentmentWrite down your worries of last week, last month and a year ago; check which were realisticOVERCOME RESENTMENT"I will try to reduce my anxieties. I will set constructive goals. I want self-respect."238–239
6Getting Rid of EmptinessWrite goals on a pad and weigh each — or close your eyes and search for themNO EMPTINESS"Time is my treasure and I will not waste it."239–240
7OpportunityRemember Sam-I-am in Green Eggs and Ham; each morning list today's opportunities and plot your courseOPPORTUNITY"What opportunities await me today? How can I make today a good day?"240–241
8ForgivenessDraw an arrow, "From forgiveness to self-respect"; picture a torture scene and see your self-torture as no kinderFORGIVENESS"I will try to forgive myself. I will try to forgive others. We are all human and imperfect."241–242
9Creative Mirror WatchingLook at yourself honestly, walk away, then come back and look at yourself as at a specific good friendFRIEND"I will be a friend to myself. I will look at myself with kind eyes."242–243
10Tearing Down Prison WallsImagine batting against a tough pitcher: stop swinging for home runs, shorten your grip and aim for a singlePRISON"I do not deserve to imprison myself… Instead, I will accept myself."243–245
11TranquillityClose your eyes and listen to the sounds that soothe you — rain on city streets, the ocean, musicTRANQUILLITY"I will be tranquil today."245
12No Retirement ClockListen to a ticking clock, real or imagined: time is your lifeCLOCK"I must not resort to 'killing' time."245–246
13IntegrityList your proud moments and your qualities of integrity; give yourself a fair shakeINTEGRITYKind eyes; see myself at my best; forgive myself; forgive others246–247
14SurvivalImagine facing 125,000 people in a stadium, so nervous you brush your teeth with soapSURVIVAL"I will accept myself no matter what all these people think of me."247–248
