Maltz Complete Ideas: Stacked by Theme + a 12-Week Application System

Across his books Maltz teaches one system, repeated in different words: 70 ideas that fall into 7 themes. You don't need all of them at once. Learn the themes in order, practise each for about three weeks, and the whole system takes 12 weeks (see "The 12-week system"). Run it day to day in the Practice Engine.

The books (codes used below): PC Psycho-Cybernetics (1960) · CLT Creative Living for Today (1967) · CF The Conquest of Frustration (1969) · PCSF Psycho-Cybernetics & Self-Fulfillment (1970) · PCY Power Psycho-Cybernetics for Youth · SSR The Search for Self-Respect (1973) · LBF Live and Be Free Thru Psycho-Cybernetics (1975).

Page numbers for the self-image come from the Self-Image Ladder. Some chapter references for the other themes were written from memory and are not yet checked against the books.

The 7 themes and how they connect

# Theme The one question it answers Role in the system
1 Self-Image Who do I believe I am? The thermostat: sets your limit
2 Success Mechanism How does my mind reach goals? The engine: steers toward the target you give it
3 Imagination How do I change the picture? The tool: rewrites the self-image
4 Beliefs & Dehypnosis What false ideas are holding me? The cleanup: removes wrong programming
5 Relaxation How do I let the engine work? The condition: change happens relaxed, not forced
6 Emotions How do I handle bad feelings and old hurts? The dashboard: warning lights + repairs
7 Living it What does the new me look like day to day? The result: confident, goal-directed living

The whole system in one sentence: Picture the real you clearly, give your mind a goal, relax and let it steer, use bad feelings as course-correction signals, and prove the new picture with small daily wins.

Theme 1: The Self-Image (the thermostat)

You always act like the person you believe you are, so this is the master key: change it and behaviour changes with much less willpower.

1.4 The plastic surgery puzzle Some patients changed after surgery, others still felt ugly: the inner scar mattered more than the outer one PC intro, ch.1
1.5 The ceiling / area of the possible You can't perform consistently beyond your self-image (the $5,000 salesman) PC 13, 50–51; LBF 35
1.6 Comfort zone Acting outside your self-image causes tension that pulls you back LBF 36
1.7 Overall average The self-image is an average of how you see yourself in many areas (work, social, money, body) LBF 35, 83
1.8 Real size, not inflated The goal is an accurate picture of your real self: not smaller, not an arrogant fantasy PC 10, 43

Memory line: I don't rise to my goals. I act at the level of who I think I am.

Your mind and nervous system work like a goal-seeking machine: give it a clear target and it steers there by itself, correcting errors along the way.

Memory line: Give the machine a clear target. Correct, don't condemn.

Theme 3: Imagination (the tool that rewrites the picture)

Imagination is how you change the self-image: vivid mental rehearsal gets stored almost like real experience.

# Idea In plain words Where

3.2 The free-throw experiment Imagined practice improved shooting about as much as real practice (23% vs 24%) PC 35

3.4 Build new "memories" Imagine how you'd feel if you already were that person; it creates success memories to draw on PC 46

Theme 4: Beliefs & Dehypnosis (the cleanup)

Many of your limits are ideas you accepted without checking, from parents, teachers, past failures or your own repetition. Rational questioning breaks them.

# Idea In plain words Where
4.1 Everyone is hypnotized a little We act on ideas absorbed uncritically from others or repeated to ourselves PC 53
4.2 Belief, not ability, limits you Lecky's students failed because of an inadequate self-image, not low aptitude PC 5
4.3 The four questions Is there a rational reason for this belief? Could I be wrong? Would I conclude this about someone else in my position? Why act as if it's true? PC 72
4.4 "I failed" vs "I am a failure" Use the verb, not the noun. Mistakes don't define you PC 5, 164, 166
4.5 Don't measure by others' norms Inferiority comes from comparing yourself to someone else's standard; you are simply "you" PC 56–58
4.6 Inferiority and superiority are one coin Both come from comparison; drop the measuring PC 57
4.7 Borrowed self-images A parent's or spouse's judgment can become your self-image; notice whose voice it is and talk back CF 20–21; PCY 169
4.8 Use rational thinking on the conscious level Your conscious mind's job is to choose goals, check facts and feed the machine correct data, not to force results PC ch.5
4.9 Desire + belief, not willpower Beliefs change the way they were formed, through experience and imagination, in a relaxed state PC 59–60

Memory line: A belief I never checked is not a fact. Question it.

The success mechanism works best when you are relaxed. Strain and over-trying jam it, so relaxation is part of the method, not a break from it.

5.2 Daily physical relaxation practice Sit or lie quietly and picture your body getting heavy and loose PC ch.4
5.3 The quiet room / decompression chamber Keep a calm room in your mind; go there for a few minutes before a stressful task PC ch.13
5.4 Responses are yours to choose Problems don't make you tense; your response does. You can choose not to react PC 157, ch.13
5.5 Delayed reaction Pause before responding to a stimulus, like letting a ringing phone ring PC ch.13
5.6 Answer only the problem in front of you One thing at a time, like sand through an hourglass PC ch.13
5.7 Stop over-carefulness Too much self-monitoring causes the "stage fright" jam; let spontaneity happen PC ch.11
5.8 Do your worrying before you act Plan consciously, then act without second-guessing PC ch.11

Memory line: Plan with effort. Perform relaxed.

The 12-week system

Pick one life area as your lab (for example: interview confidence) and run 4 phases of 21 days each, matching Maltz's 21-day minimum. Each phase adds themes on top of the last, so by week 12 you use the whole system on one real problem.

1. See the picture 1–3 1 Self-Image + 4 Beliefs Write your current self-image in your lab area. Run the four questions (4.3) on one "I can't" belief. Theater of the Mind as your real self, including the obstacle and how you handle it Old vs new picture written from memory, one belief dropped, recall test on Themes 1 + 4 Self-Image Ladder, Self-Image Day
2. Start the engine 4–6 2 Success Mechanism + 3 Imagination One goal each morning at the edge of your self-image, as an if-then plan. Rehearse it with the obstacle. Evening: 3 wins 21 if-then goals, about 60 wins, recall test on Themes 2 + 3 The Machine, Self-Fulfillment guide
3. Calm and repair 7–9 5 Relaxation + 6 Emotions Quiet room before stressful tasks. Name any FAILURE signal plus its if-then fix. Forgive one old hurt One scar forgiven, fewer FAILURE signals, recall test on Themes 5 + 6 Search for Self-Respect activity, Conquest of Frustration activity
4. Live it 10–12 7 Living it Winning feeling before tasks. One courage action per week. Weekly SUCCESS score A real-world test, a 2-minute teach-back with the book closed, recall on all 7 themes Self-Image Code podcast, Peak

Rules that keep it working

  1. Don't add a new book or theme mid-phase. Finish the 21 days first.
  2. Missed a day? Correct, don't condemn (2.4). One missed day doesn't stop a habit forming; being very inconsistent does.
  3. Judge each phase on day 21, but expect a behaviour to need about 2 months or more to feel automatic. Maltz's 21 days is a minimum, not a finish line.
  4. Never re-read to review. Close the pages and recall, then check.
  5. After 12 weeks, keep the daily routine and pick a second lab area.

WOOP in 4 lines (use it for every goal):

  1. Wish: what I want in my lab area this week
  2. Outcome: the best result, pictured vividly (Maltz)
  3. Obstacle: what in me gets in the way (a fear, a habit, a belief)
  4. Plan: If [obstacle], then I will [action].

Daily 20-minute routine + weekly review

Morning (5 min)

  • Write today's one goal as an if-then plan, at the edge of my self-image
  • Read my new-picture sentence once

Before any stressful moment (2 min)

  • Quiet room: three slow breaths and a calm place in my mind
  • Recall one past win and its feeling

Night (13 min)

  • 3 wins from today, written down
  • Any FAILURE signal? Name it and write its if-then plan
  • 3-question recall: write 3 Maltz ideas from memory, then check
  • 7 min Theater of the Mind: tomorrow's goal done as the real me, including the obstacle and my if-then response

Weekly review (Sunday, 15 min)

  1. Which phase and day am I on?
  2. What changed in my lab area this week? One concrete example from real life.
  3. Which belief or feeling kept pulling me back? Run the four questions (4.3), then write a new WOOP.
  4. Recall test: write every idea I remember from this phase's themes with the pages closed, then check.
  5. Teach-back: explain this phase's themes out loud in 2 minutes, book closed.
