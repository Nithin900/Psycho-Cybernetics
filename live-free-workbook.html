Your daily routine

This puts the whole chapter into about 15 minutes a day. Do it in this order.

  1. Morning: breathe (2 min)Breathe slowly. Tell yourself you are making yourself strong enough to step over yesterday's fears.
  2. Morning: mirror (1 min)Look at yourself and ask, “Am I a friend to myself?” Answer kindly.
  3. Morning: read the pledge (2 min)Read the daily pledge below out loud. Tick it in the tracker.
  4. Leaving home: close the doorAs you shut the door, shut it on one old mistake. Leave it behind.
  5. During the day: one thing at a timeWhen you feel rushed, stop and pick the one next thing. At a red light, wait calmly and expect the green.
  6. When a feeling comes: name itUse one sentence from “Say what you feel.” Say it to yourself or to the person.
  7. Evening: quiet time (10–30 min)Sit alone and relax. Look at your day from a distance. Throw out one worry with the trash.

The 12 tools in this chapter

Each tool shows the book page, what it means, what to do, and words you can say.

1. The daily pledge

p. 152–153 · read every morning for 21 days
Means

Every day costs you one day of your life. So you choose on purpose what you trade it for.

Do

Read this out loud each morning. Mark the day. The book asks for 21 days in a row.

Today is a new day, and it is mine to use. What I do today matters. I pay for it with one day of my life. Tomorrow this day is gone. Only what I trade it for stays. I promise myself to trade it for something good, so I don't regret the price. I think calmly and cheerfully. I am friendly to people. I am patient with people's faults, and I see what they do in the kindest way I can. I act as if my goals are sure to happen. I already am the person I want to be. I don't let gloomy talk or negative thinking change my judgment. I smile several times a day. I respond calmly, whatever happens. When I can't control something, I still respond in a positive way.
Result

The book promises less worry, guilt and anger, and more self-confidence.

This is a plain-English version. The book's exact words are on p. 152–153.

2. The person in the mirror

p. 154–155 · poem
Means

The opinion that matters most in your life is your own. You can fool other people and get praise, but if you can't look yourself in the eye, the praise is empty. If you are your own friend, you pass life's hardest test.

Do

The book asks you to say the poem with “I, me, my.” In your own words: “The person whose opinion counts most is me. I want to be someone I respect.”

Say

“Can I look myself in the eye about this?”

3. Mirror practice

p. 156–157 · every morning
Means

You are not talking to a mirror. You are talking to the most important person in your life, which is you. You need to be your own friend before you can be a good friend to others.

Do

Breathe for a few minutes. Picture the air feeding your body and your spirit. Then look in the mirror and ask, “Am I a friend to myself?” Let go of yesterday's mistake so you can respect yourself today.

Say

“Yesterday is finished. Today I'm on my own side.”

4. Throw out mental garbage

p. 158 · 6 days
Means

Your mind collects junk too: old hurts, worries, regrets.

Do

Take out the trash at home for six days. Each time, name one thought you are throwing away with it.

Say

“I'm throwing out the thought that ___.”

5. Close the door on the past

p. 158 · 6 days
Means

A past mistake doesn't get to walk into your new day.

Do

Every time you leave home and shut the door, picture shutting it on one failure or mistake.

Say

“That happened. It stays behind this door.”

6. Red light, green light

p. 159 · 6 days
Means

The red light is your fear of failing. The green light is your way forward. Nothing holds you back forever. When one door shuts, another opens.

Do

At every red light, walking or driving, wait and expect the green. When it turns green, move forward and remember a past success.

Say

“This is a pause, not a stop.”

7. Ten rules for success

p. 160–161

Easy to understand, hard to follow. Rules 3, 4 and 5 are about speaking and listening.

  1. Do one thing at a time.Two things at once get less done than one.
  2. Know the problem.Say the problem clearly before you look for answers.
  3. Listen to learn.Open your ears before you open your mouth.
  4. Ask questions.Socrates became wise by asking. Ask, even just to check.
  5. Be brief.Be known for clear, short words, not for talking a lot.
  6. Accept change.You can't step in the same river twice. Everything can improve.
  7. Admit your mistakes.Don't make excuses, and don't fear mistakes.
  8. Be simple.If a child can understand it, an adult can too.
  9. Be calm.Good judgment grows in quiet, not in a storm.
  10. Smile.Humor gives you warmth. A smile passes from person to person.

10. How to make decisions

p. 166
Means

Worry fades when you face the facts. A problem that is stated well is half solved.

Do
  • What am I worried about?
  • What can I do about it?
  • Decide what to do.
  • Start doing it right away.
Then

Once you decide and act, stop worrying about the result. You are like a player whose bet is placed while the wheel spins. You can't control it now. “If you worry, don't do it. If you do it, don't worry.”

11. Just for today

p. 167–168 · read every day for 7 days

Unused “nows” turn into useless “thens.” Just for today, I…

  • live the next 12 hours and don't fix my whole life at once.
  • remember that everyone gets the same 24 hours.
  • don't worry about what might happen. I make things happen.
  • stop saying “if I had time.” I make time.
  • learn something useful and read something that makes me think.
  • look my best, speak gently, and am polite.
  • don't find fault with others or try to change anyone but me.
  • follow a plan, and avoid hurry and indecision.
  • do a secret good deed, do two things I don't want to do, and am not afraid to enjoy beauty.
  • have a quiet half hour alone to see my life clearly.
  • am happy.

12. Four short readings

p. 169–172 · written by students of the course
Problem

A problem that stops me might be easy for someone else. So part of the problem lives in how I see it.

Friend

I want a friend who is loyal, kind, patient and forgiving. It's best if that friend is me.

Today

What I have today comes from what I did yesterday. Tomorrow comes from what I do today. Crying over the past is useless. I work with what I have now.

Certainty

The moment when you know every answer never comes. A wise person makes a good guess with too little information, then acts with courage and confidence.

Say what you feel

This is the skill the chapter builds: notice the feeling, name it calmly, then say it simply. Rule 5 says be brief, so each line is one sentence.

Speaking frames

Make a point (be brief)“My point is ___. The reason is ___. For example, ___.”
Listen first“So you mean ___? Did I get that right?”
Ask to learn“Can you help me understand ___? What would you do?”
Disagree calmly“I see it differently. Here's why: ___.”
Apologize“I'm sorry I ___. I'll ___ next time.”
Talk to yourself (mirror)“I'm on my side. I can handle the next step.”

Swap your words

The book calls saying “I can't” and “I have to” a great mistake. The words you use shape your self-image, so change them.

Stop sayingStart saying
I can't.I haven't learned it yet. / I choose not to.
I have to.I choose to, because ___.
I'm bad at this.I'm practising this.
If I had time…I'll make time on ___.
What if it goes wrong?What can I do right now?
He makes me angry.I feel angry when ___.
I always fail.That time didn't work. I'm adjusting.
I'm not sure, so I'll wait.My best guess is ___, so I'll start.

Word list from the chapter

Useful words from these pages, with a plain meaning and a sentence to copy.

Self-image
The picture you have of who you are. “I'm building a stronger self-image.”
Attitude
Your usual way of thinking and feeling about things. “My attitude is calm today.”
Aspire
To want strongly to become something. “I aspire to be a clear speaker.”
Pessimism
Expecting the worst. “I won't let pessimism decide for me.”
Tolerant
Patient with people who are different or make mistakes. “I try to be tolerant.”
Shortcomings
Faults or weak points. “We all have shortcomings.”
Negative feedback
A signal that you are off course, used to correct yourself. “That mistake is useful feedback.”
PMA
Positive mental attitude. “I'm keeping a PMA today.”
Dehypnotize
To wake up from a false belief you accepted without checking. “I'm questioning that old belief.”
Inhibit
To hold back. “I don't inhibit my real personality.”
Succinct
Short and clear. “Let me be succinct.”
Rationalize
To make excuses that sound reasonable. “I won't rationalize my mistake.”
Inevitable
Sure to happen. “Change is inevitable.”
Indecision
Not being able to decide. “Indecision wastes my time.”
Perspective
A way of seeing a situation. “Quiet time gives me perspective.”
Verdict
A final judgment. “My own verdict matters most.”
