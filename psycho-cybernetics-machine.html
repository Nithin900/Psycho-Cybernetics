What psycho-cybernetics means

Preface · PC viii–xv, 11, 18–19
“I choose to call this new concept ‘Psycho-Cybernetics’: the principles of Cybernetics as applied to the human brain.”PC 19
Key lines from the book
  • Cybernetics “has to do with teleology—goal-striving, goal-oriented behavior of mechanical systems” PC viii
  • “Psycho-Cybernetics does not say that man is a machine. Rather, it says that man has a machine which he uses” PC 19
  • “What each of us really wants, deep down, is more LIFE” PC 11
  • The self-image is changed “not by intellect alone, nor by intellectual knowledge alone, but by ‘experiencing’” PC x
  • The book is meant “not merely to be read but to be experienced” PC xii
  • “It usually requires a minimum of about 21 days to effect any perceptible change in a mental image” PC xiii
  • “Man is by nature a goal-striving being” PC xv

The self image: your key to a better life

PC 1–14 · Preface vii
“Each of us carries about with us a mental blueprint or picture of ourselves… But it is there, complete down to the last detail.”PC 2
Key lines from the book
  • “All your actions, feelings, behavior—even your abilities—are always consistent with this self-image” PC 2
  • “The self-image can be changed… one is never too young nor too old to change his self-image” PC 3
  • Efforts at change go “to the circumference of the self, so to speak, rather than to the center” PC 3
  • At the center of the system of ideas, “the keystone… is the individual’s ‘ego ideal,’ his ‘self-image’” PC 4
  • The self-image must be “a reasonable approximation of ‘you,’ being neither more than you are, nor less than you are” PC 10
  • “The key goal-image is our Self-image” PC 13
  • “If you can remember, worry, or tie your shoe, you can succeed” PC 14

Discovering the success mechanism within you

PC 15–29
“Your brain and nervous system constitute a goal-striving mechanism which operates automatically to achieve a certain goal, very much as a self-aiming torpedo or missile seeks out its target and steers its way to it.”PC 18
Key lines from the book
  • The “subconscious mind” “is not a ‘mind’ at all, but a mechanism—a goal-striving ‘servo-mechanism’” PC 12
  • “Present it with ‘success goals’ and it functions as a ‘Success Mechanism.’ Present it with negative goals, and it operates just as impersonally… as a ‘Failure Mechanism’” PC 13
  • Animals’ goals are pre-set; “man of all creatures is more than a creature, he is also a creator” PC 16–17
  • “All that the forebrain does is to select the goal, trigger it into action by desire, and feed information to the automatic mechanism” PC 20

Imagination: the first key

PC 30–47
“Your nervous system cannot tell the difference between an imagined experience and a ‘real’ experience.”PC 32
Key lines from the book
  • “A human being always acts and feels and performs in accordance with what he imagines to be true about himself and his environment” PC 31
  • “We act, or fail to act, not because of ‘will,’ as is so commonly believed, but because of imagination” PC 31
  • “You act, and feel, not according to what things are really like, but according to the image your mind holds of what they are like” PC 34
  • “Before a person can change, he must ‘see’ himself in a new role” PC 41
  • Practice: 30 minutes a day, alone, relaxed, eyes closed; a motion-picture screen of yourself, “as vivid and as detailed as possible” PC 45–46
  • A patient overcame her fear of the dentist only when she added the smell of antiseptic and the feel of the chair PC 45–46

Dehypnotize yourself from false beliefs

PC 48–63
“Every human being is hypnotized to some extent, either by ideas he has uncritically accepted from others, or ideas he has repeated to himself.”PC 53
Key lines from the book
  • Adler, told he was “dumb in mathematics”, accepted it until he worked a problem no one else could, and “went on to become a good math student” PC 48
  • Told he cannot lift a pencil, a weight-lifter who can hoist 400 pounds “now actually cannot lift the pencil” PC 53
  • “The hypnotist merely removed this mental block” PC 54
  • “Inferiority and Superiority are reverse sides of the same coin.” “You are not ‘inferior.’ You are not ‘superior.’ You are simply ‘You’” PC 56–58
  • Beliefs change in a relaxed state, the way they were formed, not by effort or will power PC 59–60

Remove emotional scars: an emotional face lift

PC 149–167
“You must become your own plastic surgeon.”PC 159

  • “To live creatively, we must be willing to be a little vulnerable” PC 166

Do-it-yourself tranquilizers

PC 187–203

Key lines from the book

  • Old Faithful: picture emotional steam “coming out the top of my head and evaporating harmlessly” PC 195

Turn a crisis into a creative opportunity

PC 204–222
“A ‘crisis’ is a situation which can either make you or break you.”PC 205
Key lines from the book
  • To perform well in a crisis: (1) practice without pressure; (2) react aggressively, not defensively, keeping your positive goal in mind; (3) evaluate the situation in its true perspective PC 206
  • “If you react properly to the situation, a ‘crisis’ can give you strength, power, wisdom you do not ordinarily possess” PC 205–206
  • Ask, “What is the worst that can possibly happen if I fail?” PC 221

That winning feeling

PC 223–244
When your creative mechanism is “set for success” you experience “that winning feeling.”PC 224
Key lines from the book
  • Relive any past success, however small, in full detail: sights, sounds, how you felt. Then picture the coming task as if you had already succeeded PC 233–234
  • “I never take counsel of my fears” (General Patton). Failure feelings originate in your own mind, so you are free to accept or ignore them PC 236
  • Engrams strengthen with use and weaken with disuse, so concentrate on the happy and pleasant PC 242–243
  • You don’t bang the phonograph; you change the record PC 243
