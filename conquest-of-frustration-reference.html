The Conquest of Frustration, at a glance

Look things up: the Twelve Faces of Frustration beside the Twelve Faces of Success, the roadblocks and other lists, Barker's statements to say aloud, every exercise and the stories. Book content only, with page numbers.

Six parts (pp. 3–176). In each, Maltz writes first and Barker, a Religious Science minister, answers. The scanned copy ends partway through Barker's last section, at p. 176.

The twelve faces

Maltz lists the Twelve Faces of Frustration in Part I (pp. 14–15) and answers each with a Face of Success in Part VI (pp. 165–168).

No.Face of frustration (pp. 14–15)Face of success (pp. 165–168)
1You are not true to yourselfTruth: know, be and forgive yourself; "greatness consists in trying to be great" (Camus)
2You use imagination destructively — no goalImagination-plus: a goal within your capabilities
3You don't know how to relaxRelaxation: the four principles; one thing at a time; sleep on it, not with it
4Your aim is unhappinessThe aim of happiness — and the duty to share it
5Frustration is a habitGood habits: "all habits are forms of self-hypnosis"
6You don't accept your weaknessesAccept your weaknesses: a floor to stand on
7You have no compassionCompassion, "the basis of successful creative living"
8You wear a maskUnmasking: no more emotional sunglasses
9You repeat your mistakesLive through your mistakes: nobody is champion every day
10You retire from lifeNever retire: "I'm eighty-two and I'm practicing to be ninety"
11You consider yourself a loserConsider yourself a winner: call on past successes
12You don't accept yourself; you live in fantasyAccept yourself for what you are; choose confidence
