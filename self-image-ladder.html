1First principles

What is the self-image made of?

Start from one dot. Each tap grows one part Maltz names, from the center out.

Its size

In Dalí’s painting for Maltz it is either “a shrunken image” or “very tall” CLT 117–118; “the size of a small potato” or “ten feet tall” PCSF 204. A three-foot bellhop’s “self-image is as tall as that twenty-six-foot Christmas tree” SSR 223.

Its right size

“A reasonable approximation of ‘you,’ being neither more than you are, nor less than you are” PC 10. Not “a fictitious self which is all-powerful, arrogant, egoistic” PC 43.

What it is not

“There is no color to a self-image” PCY 203. It is invisible but “as real as the chair you sit on” PCSF 22.

Its other names

Your “best friend” CLT 10 · “your emotional and spiritual thermostat” CF 106 · your “pacemaker” CLT 52 · conscience: “your opinion of yourself as you look in the mirror” CF 70 · “the blueprint of what you think of yourself… the road map of where you want to move” PCY 206.

3Trade-offs

Too small, too big, or real size? And where to aim?

Slide the self-image, then slide the goal. Maltz says what each choice costs.

costs Willpower and patches

“‘Positive thinking’ cannot be used effectively as a patch or a crutch to the same self image” PC 4. When incompatible with the self-image, “it absolutely will not work” LBF 37.

pays Change the center

“Once the concept of self is changed, other things consistent with the new concept of self, are accomplished easily and without strain” PC 4. “When the self-image is changed, everything else is affected” LBF 38.

costs Imitating others

“In trying to imitate the President, out of respect, I betrayed my self-image” CLT 54. “Nourish your self-image, not someone else’s” CF 168.

pays Building on happiness

“You build your self-image not on vanity or money—but on happiness” CF 71. “You will not be a success if you make a million dollars but leave your self-image impoverished” CF 163.

4Second-order effects

What builds it, what shrinks it, and what follows

Tap any factor. Watch the self-image change and read the knock-on effect.
Second-order effects Maltz names
  • The cycle: experience then seems to “prove” the image, setting up “a vicious or a beneficent cycle” PC 2–3
  • “If your self-image is weak, your positive qualities do not matter—they’re irrelevant; you will find ways to torture yourself. Nothing you do will be good enough” CLT 52
  • “Our self-image and our habits tend to go together. Change one and you will automatically change the other” PC 108; “Your habits are spontaneously altered when you change your self-image” LBF 143–144
  • “Our feelings about ourselves tend to correspond to our feelings about other people” PC 120
  • “Emptiness is symptomatic of a weak self-image” CLT 86
  • “The two major pitfalls of people: (1) withdrawal from life and (2) reliance upon factors outside of one’s concept of one’s own worth” CLT 61
  • Most people’s self-images are “reluctant, reluctant to change” CLT 141

7Deliberate practice

Your problem → Maltz’s solution

Pick the problem you have today. Get Maltz’s fix, the steps, and a 21-day counter.

Why 21 days

“It usually requires a minimum of about 21 days to effect any perceptible change in a mental image.” So “reserve judgment—and go on practicing—for a minimum period of 21 days” PC xiii–xiv. “The building of an adequate self image is something that should continue throughout a lifetime” PC xiv.
